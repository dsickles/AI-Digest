**Is a weekly run of this size safe?** Yes, with a lot of room. 67 calls is under 7% of the daily cap, and the busiest minute was under half the per-minute cap, even with batches of up to 10 parallel calls. A fuller weekly run (paging every sweep, quotes and replies for 3 posts on all 14 anchors) would land around 150 to 250 calls, still well under the cap. The real cost was not rate limits but payload size: one `get_posts_by_ids` call for 13 posts returned 284 KB because the connector adds media and article expansions. Ask only for the fields needed and keep id batches small.

## 9. Verdict

**Worth it:**
- **Missed stories.** This was by far the best return. Lab and government accounts plus a few watchlist researchers surfaced at least 5 stories that should have been in W43: the OpenAI firings, OpenAI's training-containment escape and Brotli bug, Anthropic and AISI on Mythos 5, Claude formal verification, and OpenAI's chip-controls retreat. Our feeds missed all of them.
- **Named reactions on the safety and security stories.** METR's two reviews and the PHP story drew substantive, first-hand reactions from researchers, including people who worked on the engagement.
- **Seeing the other side of a debate** our feeds only show one side of (cyber offense vs defense, and the industry critique of the Anthropic bill).

**Noise:**
- Quote and reply pulls: about 10 calls returned mostly reposts and low-signal replies. The named reactions were better found by searching the watchlist directly.
- Open searches without the watchlist: mostly aggregators and bots.
- Counts for broad themes (AI and jobs) and the 7-day count window, which cannot see the start of a finished week if the run is more than a day late.

**What I'd change in the plan:**
1. Put lab and government accounts (AnthropicAI, OpenAI, GoogleDeepMind, AISecurityInst, METR) in a sixth `primary-sources` group and sweep them first. That is where the missed stories came from.
2. Run on Sunday morning, as the weekly digest does, so `get_posts_counts_recent` still covers the whole week.
3. Pull quotes only for posts whose quote count is high relative to likes, and skip reply pulls unless a post is clearly argued. That cuts calls and noise.
4. Allow a one-day lead-in (Saturday before the week) for reactions to stories that broke just before Sunday, like the Anthropic bill.
5. Rebuild the edtech and jobs parts of the watchlist. Those themes got almost no watchlist voices this week.
6. Rate limits are not the constraint at this size. Keep a per-run budget of about 250 calls and keep logging every call.
